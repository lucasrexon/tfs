TFS research · 09:25 short on +50% gappers · Nov 2024 to Nov 2026

What news and industry say about each short

Verdict

    The one filter that passed: test year, with and without it

    2.4% of the account per short, at most 3 open, cover at −20% or the next day's close, stop at 3× the entry. "Typical" is the middle of 300 simulated years with a random pick among same-day gappers; "bad luck" is the 5th worst in 100.

    Method

    How each trade's news was found

    Every gapper was matched with Massive news headlines (Benzinga and GlobeNewswire) and with the company's own press releases filed on SEC EDGAR as 8-K or 6-K exhibits. Each item was dated: by 9:25 (the bot can use it), the 4 days before, or later that day (it explains the gap but came too late for this bot). Keyword rules read each release's headline first and sort it into one of 15 types. Whether the company was being bought out or doing the buying was decided by matching the company's own name in the headline.

    Patterns

    Every group, labelled by how much to trust it

    strongSurvives the correction for testing about 60 groups at once (q below 0.05) and points the same way in both years. No group reached this. moderateLess than a 5% chance of a gap this big between the group and the rest by luck alone (p below 0.05), and the same direction in both years. weakSame direction in both years, but could easily be luck. unreliableBetter in one year and worse in the other. too fewUnder 15 trades, or under 5 in one of the years.

    Filters

    Which skip rules hold up on a year they did not see

    Each rule looks at one year and skips every group whose trades lost money on average there ("avg below zero"), or only groups clearly worse than the rest ("clearly worse"). Then it is scored on the other year, and again the other way round. A rule passes only if it raised the typical year and improved both bad luck and the worst drop, in both directions.
